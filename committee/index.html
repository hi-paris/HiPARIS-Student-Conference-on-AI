The conference is run by a student committee, supervised by a senior academic committee of faculty members. Being a student conference, most committee members are students — and may themselves submit. The rules below explain how decisions are made and how conflicts of interest are handled so that the process stays fair.

Senior academic committee

Luiz Chamon and Aymeric Dieuleveut.

The senior academic committee supervises the whole process: it validates the rules of the conference, arbitrates sensitive cases escalated by the chairs, takes part in the final program decisions, and decides all awards on the basis of the reviews and decisions.

Area chairs

Mounir Lbath, Clément Meyer, Théophile Faure (final list to be confirmed).

Each area chair follows a set of submissions (around eight) through the review process. Area chairs do not write reviews themselves: they assign and monitor reviewers, lead the discussion among them, consolidate the reviews into a recommendation, and propose the decision for each submission they follow. Together with the senior academic committee, they take the final decisions on the program (posters and orals).

How decisions are made

The pipeline is: submission → automatic checks → double-blind review → discussion → decision → final version → publication.

  1. Each submission receives at least two reviews (every submitting author also reviews two submissions), following the public criteria of the reviewer guidelines.
  2. The area chair in charge of the submission leads the discussion among reviewers and consolidates a recommendation.
  3. Accept/reject decisions and the program (posters and orals) are taken collectively by the area chairs together with the senior academic committee.
  4. Awards are decided solely by the senior academic committee, based on the reviews and decisions.

Conflicts of interest

Committee members are students and may submit to the conference — their submissions go through the normal review process. Fairness is preserved by strict conflict rules:

  • No one ever handles their own submission. An area chair who is an author of — or in conflict with — a submission (co-author, same project group, close collaborator) is excluded from its review, which is assigned to another area chair.
  • Reviewers declare their conflicts when they are assigned submissions, and we avoid same-school assignments when the reviewer pool allows it.
  • Review is double blind: reviewers do not know who the authors are, and commit to not trying to find out.
  • Conflicts involving the chairs themselves are arbitrated by the senior academic committee; the ethics chair can be contacted confidentially at any point.
  • Awards are decided by the senior academic committee only — faculty members who do not compete — so student chairs competing in the conference have no influence on prizes.
